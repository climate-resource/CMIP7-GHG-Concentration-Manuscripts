\section{Output dataset requirements and implications thereof}
\label{sec:output-dataset-requirements}

As a result of their specific use case, the requirements of CMIP forcings are unique.
These requirements affect the delivery timelines, the level of accuracy required,
spatial and temporal characteristics of the data.

The first requirement is to work within CMIP's overall delivery timeline.
This timeline is, by scientific standards, relatively strict,
which means that forcings producers have to produce data and make it available to modelling teams quickly
and that the benefit of improvements must be weighed against any delays that would result from switching to the improved datasets.
For example, this paper is being released after the data has already been made available to modelling teams.
Releasing it before providing data to modelling teams would be the more natural order,
but this would introduce an unacceptable delay to the overall process
(at least for greenhouse gas concentration forcings,
other forcings, e.g. [ZNTODO solar ref Funke et al.] have managed to work in the more natural order).
The result of this constraint is that some identified bugs are not fixed:
this is a clear example of a pragmatic balance being struck
between fixing identified errors and beginning ESM simulations.
Sometimes the need to start simulations means that identified bugs are not fixed
because the cost of the fix, i.e. restarting simulations, is not worth the benefit.

The second requirement is to meet CMIP's overall experiment design.
CMIP7 includes a number of different experiments.
Ideally, forcings datasets would support all experiments.
In practice, forcings datasets are produced for the historical, piControl and scenario experiments,
with all other experiments only requiring (user-created) combinations and variations thereof.
Here we only focus on the historical and piControl experiments:
the scenario experiments are considered in [ZNTODO cite in-prep paper].
Supporting the historical and piControl experiments drives the temporal requirements of the dataset.
The historical experiment starts in 1850 and, for CMIP7, ends in 2021-12, so the dataset must start in 1850 and extend to at least 2021-12.
The piControl dataset must also facilitate a multi-century simulation with 1850-like conditions.
The other requirement introduced by the experimental design is that there should only be one forcing dataset.
In other words, forcing dataset producers must produce a single, best-estimate dataset,
rather than being able to produce a number of versions that span the uncertainty in their forcing dataset.

The third requirement is that the data must support use as an input to ESM simulations.
This means that the data must be complete i.e. it may not contain any missing values for data points required by ESMs.
All required temporal values must be included, or able to be derived trivially (e.g. via linear interpolation),
and spatial coverage must be complete, which usually means globally complete
but can also mean e.g. `over land areas' for datasets which are only defined on land (e.g. land-use forcings).
This requirement is key to standardisation: if there were missing values,
then each modelling team would have to make their own interpolation choice
(a model cannot have a missing value as an input),
which would introduce the risk that each ESM team makes a slightly different choice
(thus failing to provide the desired standardisation).

The use within ESM simulations also has implications for the accuracy of the data.
Differences in energy balance of <0.01~W/m^2 have almost zero impact on climate simulations.
As a result, this threshold can be used to identify higher priority areas or choices
when constructing the dataset
(if a choice changes the dataset in a way that alters the energy balance by less than 0.01~W/m^2,
it is usually not a critical choice because the difference betwen the options is effectively zero).

Taken together, these considerations define the output requirements for this study:
a temporally (at least from 1850 to end of 2021),
spatially (global coverage) complete dataset
that is appropriate for ESM simulations
(choices which alter the implied energy balance by more than 0.01~W / m2,

i.e. around 1~ppm CO_2, should be carefully considered and documented).

These requirements have some key implications.
Firstly, the requirement of completeness means that
we (and other forcings providers)
must make a number of choices to fill gaps in observational data, both spatially and temporally.
These choices usually mean that forcings will differ from the raw data products that underpin them.
These differences should not be taken as a sign that the raw data is problematic
(on the contrary, the raw data is usually the better source, if you can use it),
but are a sign that the process of creating a temporally and spatially complete data product comes with compromises.
In practice, these gaps are usually filled by including data from a number of different sources,
meaning that most forcings are composite data products.
Combining data from multiple sources comes with a number of subjective choices
for which no objective answer and a decision must simply be made.
In the best case, there is time to consider the implications of each different choice.
In the worst case, this time is not available and a choice is made,
with a thorough analysis of the pros and cons of other choices having to be left to future work.

The requirement to produce a single, best-estimate dataset further compels us and other forcings providers to make choices:
providing multiple datasets and letting users choose based on their own use case is not an option.
In practice, forcings providers produce the best-estimate data product that they can,
based on their knowledge of the underlying data products and the CMIP use case.
However, it is again important to reinforce that forcings products
should not be viewed as an authoritative set of global observations.
They are a best-estimate, suitable for the CMIP exercise,
but they do not imply that one product is better than an another
or that the numbers they provide are better
than those from other, particularly more directly observation-based, exercises.

Given their unique requirements, potential users must be aware
that CMIP forcings may not be the most appropriate dataset for their use case
or that some post-processing might be required to make the dataset work for a different use case.
In any given forcing dataset, there are likely to be underlying choices which,
were the forcings to be produced for a different context, would be different.
For the greenhouse gas forcings specifically, there are two key choices to flag.
The first is that the key application is for simulations that start in 1850.

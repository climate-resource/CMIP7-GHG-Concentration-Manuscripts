The seventh phase of the Coupled Model Intercomparison Project (CMIP7) is in progress \citep{dunne_cmip7_2025}.
Within CMIP7, there are a range of experiments.
Here we consider experiments which explore future climate change.
These experiments provide insights into the future climate change that is consistent with the forcings that define each experiment.
However, they have applications beyond this.
As discussed in [TODO cite SSP paper], there can be more than one way,
specifically set of underlying socioeconomic assumptions,
to produce forcings consistent with each experiment.
As a result, these experiments not only provide information about future climate change under the specific assumptions made in their underlying scenario,
but also future climate change in any other scenario which results in forcings that are consistent with the experiment's forcings
(where consistency is not defined here and what meets the requirements of conistency will depend on the intended use case).
This means that they are a key link point between various socioeconomic explorations
and the resulting future climate change
(this link is extremely valuable because of the cost of running an ESM: we cannot run an ESM for every set of forcings of interest,
so having ESM output for a range of forcing sets, which covers many potential variations, is key to allow for an at least approximate estimate of future climate change under any set of underlying assumptions).

Within CMIP7's exploration of possible futures, the most well known effort is ScenarioMIP [TODO other citations] \citep{van-vuuren_scenariomip_2025}.
ScenarioMIP's remit is to explore future climate change under a selected range of possible boundary conditions
(with the question of what to select being the key choice for the ScenarioMIP team to make,
given that not all possible boundary conditions can be run by ESMs).
In CMIP7, ScenarioMIP explores scenarios

that range from very low future emissions (the VL scenario) to high emissions (the H scenario),
stepping through
low followed by (very) negative emissions (LN);
low emissions (L), approximately at the top of the range that could be considered consistent with the Paris Agreement [TODO cite];
medium followed by low emissions (ML);
medium emissions (M), approximately in line with countries' current Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) [TODO cite PA];
and high followed by low (and then negative) emissions (HL) \citep[for full details see ][]{van-vuuren_scenariomip_2025} [TODO add Riahi citation here].
Beyond ScenarioMIP, other efforts are also being setup.
Here we illustrate our support for such efforts by also presenting the GHG forcings for PolMIP's [TODO check citation] VL-CF experiment.
VL-CF is a counterfactural experiment in which emissions are reduced from the start of 2016 onwards (i.e. just after the signing of The Paris Agreement)
such that global-mean temperatures peak at around 1.5~\textdegree C (according to AR6 science).

There is an increasing move towards emissions-driven capability in ESMs.
At this stage, CO_2 emissions-driven capability is close to mainstream,
while some early adopters also have CH_4 and N_2O emissions-driven capability.
For these teams, the CO_2, CH_4 and N_2O concentrations provided here are not needed.
However, for all other teams, there is still a reliance on our provided concentrations.
For species other than CO_2, CH_4 and N_2O, all models will rely on our provided GHG concentration forcings as far as we are aware.
The production of GHG concentration forcings for all species also facilitates concentration-driven experiments,
which can be key to understanding uncertainties in modelling of Earth's carbon cycle.

Here we describe how the GHG concentration forcings are produced for CMIP7's future-focussed experiments.
We present the datasets which are already available (specifically ScenarioMIP's experiments and PolMIP's VL-CF experiment)
and note that the production of any other dataset used in CMIP7's future-focussed experiments is likely to follow the same methodology.

We do not document how to use these forcings in each experiment.
As noted in \citet{nicholls_historical-ghgs_2026}, such documentation is best left to the CMIP7 guidance pages
because these pages are kept up to date in real time
(see \newline \mbox{\url{https://wcrp-cmip.github.io/cmip7-guidance/docs/CMIP7/Experiment_set_up_and_Forcings/}})
or papers describing the experiments themselves
(although these are scientific documents so cannot be easily updated
so our recommendation is still to treat the guidance pages as the most reliable source of up-to-date information).
The GHG forcings described here will also be used in a number of other experiments that we do not enumerate here.
As above, please see the guidance pages at
\newline \mbox{\url{https://wcrp-cmip.github.io/cmip7-guidance/docs/CMIP7/Experiment_set_up_and_Forcings/}}
or the papers describing the experiments for details about which forcings to use
and how to implement them in your model.

As is also noted in \citet{nicholls_historical-ghgs_2026}, how to record which forcings were used in a simulation
(e.g. how to choose the forcing index, i.e. the `f' in variant labels like `r1i1p1f1')
is not yet settled.
Users should consult the introduction of the guidance pages \newline
(\mbox{\url{https://wcrp-cmip.github.io/cmip7-guidance/docs/CMIP7/Experiment_set_up_and_Forcings/}}),
check back for updates frequently and use the guidance pages as the primary source of information.
Following \newline \mbox{\url{https://github.com/PCMDI/input4MIPs_CVs/issues/415}}
may also help track discussions based on a concrete use case.
Recommendations that appear in papers, including this one,
should only be used as a secondary source, because they can quickly become outdated.
If the recommendations are still unclear or not defined when users are producing their model output files,
we encourage the teams to throughly document their `f' mapping so that this information
can be provided to users once an agreed communication format has been agreed.

In line with the historical concentrations \citep{nicholls_historical-ghgs_2026},
all of our values are mole fractions in dry air.
However, throughout, we use `concentration' as shorthand.
As noted in \citet{nicholls_historical-ghgs_2026},
our output files use CF standard names of the form \texttt{mole\_fraction\_of\_X\_in\_air}
(e.g. \texttt{mole\_fraction\_of\_carbon\_dioxide\_in\_air}, \citealp{eaton_cf-conventions_2026}).
The CF standard names do not state whether these \texttt{\_in\_air} names include the effect of water vapour.
For CO_2, CH_4 and N_2O, there are standard names of the form \texttt{mole\_fraction\_of\_X\_in\_dry\_air},
which are explicit, so there is likely a metadata error here (see Sect.~\ref{sec:discussion}).
Regardless of any metadata error, our values are dry-air mole fractions, consistent with the historical dataset.

The output datasets are provided at different spatial and temporal resolutions.
We provide both monthly and yearly datasets,
as well as global-means, hemispheric-means and datasets that include latitudinal variations.
No dependency on longitude is provided.
For a brief discussion on how add a vertical dimension to these datasets, see Sect.~[TODO number] of \citet{nicholls_historical-ghgs_2026}.

The output datasets extend from year 2022 to the end of 2500.
The CMIP7 historical experiment runs to the end of 2021,
so this means that users can use the scenario datasets from the end of their historical simulations
(2022 is also included in the historical datasets, but users should use the scenario datasets for 2022 values where they can
although the difference between the two is negligible).
The output data is split by experiment
and into `main' (up until the end of 2100) and extensions (2101 to the end of 2025).
As above, see the guidance pages \newline
(\mbox{\url{https://wcrp-cmip.github.io/cmip7-guidance/docs/CMIP7/Experiment_set_up_and_Forcings/}})
for specific information about how to find and use the datasets needed for each experiment.
The set of GHGs made available matches the historical set \citep{nicholls_historical-ghgs_2026}
and the set provided for CMIP6's ScenarioMIP experiments \citep{meinshausen_ssp-ghgs_2020},
including the same set of equivalent species.

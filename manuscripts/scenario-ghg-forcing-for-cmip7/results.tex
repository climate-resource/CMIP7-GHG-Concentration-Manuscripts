\subsection{Note on radiative effect comparisons}
\label{ssec:results-radiative-forcing-comparisons-note}

Throughout this section, we provide comparisons between datasets.
We provide these comparisons both in units of concentration as well as in units of W/m2.
As discussed in detail in \citet{nicholls_historical-ghgs_2026},
it must be noted that the comparisons in units of W/m2 are an approximation.
The approximation matches or overestimates the true (effective) radiative forcing.

\subsection{CO_2}
\label{ssec:results-co2}

The resulting dataset transitions smoothly from the historical dataset
at both our native resolution (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-co2}a),
and hemispheric means (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-co2}b).
The global- annual-means have approximately tracked
the data available from the Indicators of Global Climate Change (IGCC) project \citep{forster_igcc_2026}.
% % value-check: {"tag": "igcc-last-year", "unit": "yr", "lower": 2025, "upper": 2025}
% % value-check: {"tag": "co2-scenariomip-minus-igcc-last-igcc-year", "unit": "ppm", "lower": -0.8, "upper": -0.6}
% the ScenarioMIP experiments are around 0.7~ppm lower than IGCC (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-co2}c, inset).
The scenarios are roughly evenly spaced until 2100
and the datasets all have one of two shapes (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-co2}c):
peak and decline (with the decline being very gentle for VL-CF)
or ongoing rise (H, M).
% TODO check values here
Post 2100, the datasets either settle between 300 and 350~ppm
or demonstrate a slow stabilisation consistent with the net-zero emissions
in the underlying emissions (Fig.~\ref{fig:emissions-co2}a).
The clear exception here is the LN scenario,
which has the equal lowest concentrations by 2100
and continues to well below pre-industrial concentrations by 2200,
before rising again once its emissions return to zero by 2300
and the system begins to re-equilibrate (Fig.~\ref{fig:emissions-co2}a).
This excursion into deeply negative emissions and below pre-industrial concentrations
is a novelty that has not previously been explored in detail with ESMs,
particularly emissions-driven ESMs.
Examining the ESM output from this experiment
will be key to informing future updates of simple climate models
and our method as it will provide new information
about the extent to which our assumptions
about the reversibility of seasonality and latitudinal gradients holds in physics-based models.

% Deliberately decide not to make any comment about when the scenarios are distinguishable.
% Separating detectable signal from noise is its own paper.

Compared to CMIP6, the highest concentration scenarios peak at much lower levels (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-co2}d).
For example, CMIP6's highest concentration scenario was SSP5-8.5, which peaked above 2000~ppm.
In contrast, CMIP7's highest concentration scenario is H, which peaks below 1500~ppm.
The medium and low concentration scenarios cover a similar range across the two CMIP phases (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-co2}e, f).
The major exception, as discussed already, is LN, which is a completely new shape and absolute value from anything in CMIP6.
A more minor exception is VL-CF, which has a significantly lower peak than any CMIP6 or CMIP7 scenario,
in line with its counterfactual nature.

\subsection{CH_4}
\label{ssec:results-ch4}

CH_4's strong seasonality and latitudinal gradient continues into the scenarios (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-ch4}a, b).
% value-check: {"tag": "igcc-last-year", "unit": "yr", "lower": 2025, "upper": 2025}
% value-check: {"tag": "ch4-scenariomip-minus-igcc-last-igcc-year", "unit": "ppb", "lower": 30, "upper": 35}
% value-check: {"tag": "ch4-history-minus-igcc-2022", "unit": "ppb", "lower": 10.5, "upper": 11.5}
The global- annual-means for the ScenarioMIP experiments are 30--35~ppb higher than IGCC (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-ch4}c, inset),
of which around 11~ppb is already there in 2022.
The difference in 2022 is due to differences between IGCC and the historical dataset \citep{nicholls_historical-ghgs_2026},
with the rest coming from the fact that we harmonise to the historical datasets' gradient (Sect.~\ref{sssec:methods-ch4-project-global-mean}).
while the IGCC dataset shows a notable change in gradient in 2023,
which provides another illustration of the difficulty of harmonisation (Sect.~\ref{sssec:methods-ch4-project-global-mean}).
The group of scenarios with lower peaks have concentrations consistent with declining emissions.
The difference in their end of century concentrations is driven by the different floor
which CH_4 emissions reach in each experiment
(Figs.~\ref{fig:results-ch4}c and \ref{fig:emissions-ch4}a).
The ML experiment's CH_4 concentrations take longer to start declining,
then show a similar shape to the lower peak scenarios,
consistent with its delayed cut in CH_4 emissions.
The M, H and HL experiments all follow a similar rise initially.
M and H then stabilise at different levels, driven by the different levels at which their emissions stabilise.
In line with its narrative and underlying emissions, concentrations in HL drop rapidly from 2070.
% TODO check values here
Post 2100, CH_4 concentrations stabilise between 900 and 1200~ppb (all experiments except M and H)
or remain at elevated levels consistent with ongoing, approximately constant CH_4 emissions (M and H).

Compared to CMIP6, the higher peak group of experiments reach similar concentrations until 2100 (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-ch4}d).
After 2100, the H scenario's CH_4 concentrations remain notably higher than any CMIP6 scenario,
reflecting the different extension choices.
The medium peak group are similar to CMIP6, with the M's much higher extension being the notable difference (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-ch4}e).
The lower peak group of scenarios has a higher peak than CMIP6 (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-ch4}f),
reflecting updated assumptions about the speed with which CH_4 mitigation can be implemented compared to CMIP6 (Fig.~\ref{fig:emissions-ch4}d).
The lower peak group also has a larger spread in peak concentrations
and different extensions that are in line with higher sustained CH_4 emissions than assumed in CMIP6.

\subsection{N_2O}
\label{ssec:results-n2o}

N_2O's moderate seasonality and latitudinal gradient continues into the scenarios (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-n2o}a, b).
% value-check: {"tag": "igcc-last-year", "unit": "yr", "lower": 2025, "upper": 2025}
% value-check: {"tag": "n2o-scenariomip-minus-igcc-last-igcc-year", "unit": "ppb", "lower": 0.15, "upper": 0.25}
The ScenarioMIP experiment datasets track the IGCC data closely (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-n2o}c, inset),
providing an illustration of where the harmonisation seems to work better in the short-term
(in contrast to CH_4, see Sect.~\ref{sec:results-ch4}).
There is a roughly even spread of N_2O concentration pathways across the CMIP7 scenarios (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-n2o}c),
albeit with a larger gap to the VL and VL-CF scenarios
that reflects the exploration of all available N_2O mitigation options in VL
(and VL-CF, which uses VL's N_2O emissions).
Post 2100, all experiments' N_2O concentrations decline back to approximately pre-industrial levels,
reflecting the emissions extensions which also return to approximately zero emissions (Fig.~\ref{fig:emissions-n2o}a).

Compared to CMIP6, the concentrations are very similar out to 2100 (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-n2o}d, e, f).
The exception is VL, which explores novel N_2O mitigation, compared to CMIP6 too.
Beyond 2100, CMIP6 and CMIP7 are notably different.
While CMIP6 assumed ongoing emissions, related to agriculture in particular \citep[see Fig.~\ref{fig:emissions-n2o}b--d and][]{meinshausen_ssp-ghgs_2020},
CMIP7 assumes that all N_2O emissions can be mitigated.
There is no correct answer for these highly uncertain long-term extensions
but the impact of the two different choices is made clear here.

\subsubsection{CFC-12-eq}
\label{sssec:results-equivalence-datasets-cfc12eq}

CFC-12-eq's seasonality and latitudinal gradient continues from the historical datasets'
and looks unusual because it is the composite of multiple species' seasonality and latitudinal gradients (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-cfc12eq}a, b).
In line with the input data sources, CFC-12-eq's ongoing decline is projected to continue in the future (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-cfc12eq}c).

The changes from CMIP6 are minor (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-cfc12eq}d, e, f).
Present-day concentrations are slightly lower than those used in CMIP6,
largely because of a change in the radiative efficiencies used to convert to CFC-12-eq
rather than a change in the actual concentrations of each gas \citep{nicholls_historical-ghgs_2026}.

\subsubsection{HFC-134a-eq}
\label{sssec:results-equivalence-datasets-hfc134aeq}

HFC-134a-eq's almost zero seasonality, but notable latitudinal gradient,
continues from the historical datasets' (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-hfc134aeq}a, b).
Compared to the other species, projections of HFC-134a-eq are less consistent with the underlying scenario narratives and show more unusual shapes.
This is in line with the underlying emissions (Fig.~\ref{fig:emissions-hfc134aeq}a).
The most notable features are the odd shapes of the ML and M datasets
and the fact that L has the third highest concentrations,
which points to a consistency issue across the experiment ensemble for the emissions which underpin HFC-134a-eq.
Given the small effective radiative forcing of these gases compared to other forcers,
these inconsistencies are a minor problem for CMIP7 experiments (they may be more important in other contexts).
Post 2100, the extensions are consistent with an ongoing decline in emissions (Fig.~\ref{fig:emissions-hfc134aeq}a),
with the difference in concentrations being largely determined by cumulative emissions of gases with long lifetimes on the way to zero emissions.

Compared to CMIP6, the highest peak HFC-134a-eq concentration is slightly lower.
The medium peak scenarios are similar at the end of the century, but take quite different paths before then.
The lower peak scenarios are similar, with the exception of the L scenario.
The extensions in CMIP6 are generally similar, with the exception of SSP4-3.4 and SSP4-6.0,
which both remain at elevated concentrations compared to M and ML.

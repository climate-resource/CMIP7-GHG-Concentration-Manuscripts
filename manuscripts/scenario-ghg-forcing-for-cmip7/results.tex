\subsection{Note on radiative effect comparisons}
\label{ssec:results-radiative-forcing-comparisons-note}

Throughout this section, we provide comparisons between datasets.
We provide these comparisons both in units of concentration as well as in units of W/m2.
As disussed in detail in [TODO cite historical manuscript],
it must be noted that the comparisons in units of W/m2 are an approximation.
The approximation matches or overestimates the true (effective) radiative forcing.

\subsection{CO_2}
\label{ssec:results-co2}

The resulting dataset transitions smoothly from the historical dataset
at both our native resolution (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-co2}a),
and hemispheric means (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-co2}b).
The scenarios are roughly evenly spaced until 2100
and the datasets all have one of two shapes (Fig.~\ref{fig:results-co2}c):
peak and decline (with the decline being very gentle for VL-CF)
or ongoing rise (H, M).
% TODO check values here
Post 2100, the datasets either settle between 350 and 400~ppm
or demonstrate a slow stabilisation consistent with the approximately net-zero emissions
in the underlying emissions ([TODO appendix figure and ref]).
The clear exception here is the LN scenario,
which has the equal lowest concentrations by 2100
and continues to well below pre-industrial concentrations by 2200,
before [TODO check emissions, I think what we see after is basically re-equilibration with net-zero emissions].
This excursion into deeply negative emissions and below pre-industrial concentrations
is a novelty that has not previously been explored in detail with ESMs,
particularly emissions-driven ESMs.
Examining the ESM output from this experiment
will be key to informing future updates of simple climate models
and our method as it will provide new information
about the extent to which our assumptions
about the reversibility of seasonality and latitudinal gradients holds in physics-based models.

% Deliberately decide not to make any comment about when the scenarios are distinguishable.
% Separating detectable signal from noise is its own paper.

Compared to CMIP6, the highest concentration scenarios peak at much lower levels (Fig.\ref{fig:results-co2}d).
For example, CMIP6's highest concentration scenario was SSP5-8.5, which peaked above 2000~ppm.
In contrast, CMIP7's highest concentration scenario is H, which peaks below 1500~ppm.
The medium and low concentration scenarios cover a similar range across the two CMIP phases (Fig.\ref{fig:results-co2}e, f).
The major exception, as discussed already, is LN, which is a completely new shape and absolute value from anything in CMIP6.
A more minor exception is VL-CF, which has a significantly lower peak than any CMIP6 or CMIP7 scenario,
in line with its counterfactual nature.
